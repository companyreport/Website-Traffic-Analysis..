The read

Views fell more than twice as fast as visitors. Views dropped 28% and visitors 13%, which means last report's depth gain has largely unwound: 1.51 views per visitor down to 1.25. The breakthrough to 1.47 lasted exactly one week. That was the week three new compliance pieces were live and being read together; without them, readers are back to taking one page and leaving.

The decay rate is the finding that matters. Three articles went from 59 views to under 12 combined in seven days. Across 269 published posts, exactly one — NASA Is Insourcing — has held a top position in five consecutive reports. Everything else spikes on publication and disappears. That is why 21 more posts this week produced the lowest daily traffic in the series rather than the highest.

Work out what is different about the NASA piece. It has run 30, 20, 45, 15 and now 12 views across five reports, and it is the only article that has ever done so. Two reports ago a visible search query — "what sub contractors are out at nasa" — showed why: it answers a specific question people type into a search box, repeatedly, independent of news cycles. The breaking-news pieces answer a question that is only asked the week the news breaks. That is the entire difference, and it is reproducible.

Discovery Call took first place and was the only page that grew. 29 views, up 7%, while the site fell 28% around it. It is also the second report running where it has climbed. Whatever is routing people to the booking page is working independently of the traffic swings, which makes it the steadiest thing on the site right now.

Attribution is now genuinely good. Untracked traffic fell to 44% from 59%, the best reading in the series and down from 70% in September. More than half your views now carry a named source. This is the one measurement problem on the list that has fixed itself without intervention.

Search fell 17% while Facebook rose 12%. Search went to 60, Facebook to 38 — Facebook now carries 35% of all identified traffic. For a site publishing 21 posts a week, search falling is the wrong direction: it is the channel that should compound as the library grows, and it is not compounding, which is the decay problem seen from the other side.

Thursday had no posts and the best reading depth. 38 views from 27 visitors at 1.41 per visitor, on a day nothing new went live. Monday published seven posts and ran 1.26. Across this period, publishing volume and reading depth were unrelated — more evidence that what is being published is not what is being read.

Saturday the 31st was a clean one-page day: 15 views from 15 visitors, exactly 1.00. That is the fourth such day across the last five reports. It was also Halloween, which likely accounts for the low count, but not for the depth.

LinkedIn held at 6 for the second report running. The sequence across eight reports is 22, 7, 7, 7, 3, 6, 6. It has been at or near this floor for six weeks and nothing has been done about it either way.

773 subscribers. Eighth consecutive report, unchanged. The site has gone from 104 posts to 269 across this series — 165 more posts — without adding one subscriber. Traffic has risen and fallen repeatedly; this number has never moved in any direction.

What to do next

Ordered by what unblocks the most.

  1. Stop publishing daily and write for search questions instead21 posts this week produced the lowest daily traffic in eight reports, because breaking-news pieces die within seven days. The NASA article survives because it answers a question people type repeatedly. Cut to two or three posts a week and make each one answer a specific recurring question — "what is Davis-Bacon", "do I need a consultant to win a contract", "how does CPARS work" — written to still be the right answer in six months.
  2. Rewrite the three decayed pieces as evergreen answersDavis-Bacon, consultant fees and the Navy settlement had real demand in week one. Rework each from a news story into a standing explainer with a plain-question headline, and cross-link them to each other and to the NASA piece. You already know people wanted these topics; the format is what expired, not the subject.
  3. Fix the subscriber capture — eighth report, 165 posts, zero subscribersOpen a live post on a desktop, where five in six of your readers are, confirm a subscribe form renders and submits, and push a test address through to GoHighLevel. Everything else on this page moves up and down. This has never moved at all.
  4. Protect the Discovery Call route29 views and the only page to grow in a week the site fell 28%, and its second consecutive rise. Find out which pages are linking to it and make sure the next template change does not break that path.
  5. Decide about LinkedIn, finallySix weeks at or near 6 views. This has been carried on the list for four reports without action. Either assign a posting cadence and measure it for two weeks, or close it out and move the effort to Facebook, which now delivers six times the traffic.
  6. Connect Search ConsoleSearch fell 17% while the library grew by 21 posts, and you cannot see which queries you rank for or which pages are decaying. Attribution has improved on its own; this is the remaining blind spot and it is the one that would show you which articles are compounding.

Brick by brick.
